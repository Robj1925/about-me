A price alert tells you a number. A tracker with a baseline tells you if the number is good. I am planning a group trip, and I handed the daily airfare check to Meta Muse so nobody in the group has to refresh flight tabs every day.

## The problem with group travel

Group trips mean several schedules, preferred routes and a budget, all at once. Fares move every day. Usually one person gets stuck checking prices each morning and guessing when to book. That person was going to be me.

## The routine

I set up Muse to run the same job every morning, on its own:

- **Check the fares.** It queries the flight prices for the trip.
- **Log the movement.** It keeps a record of how the price changes day to day.
- **Keep a baseline.** It calculates a rolling 7 day average from that record.
- **Send the call.** It pushes a summary to my phone that says whether today's fare is above or below the average, with a buy now or hold off recommendation.

## Why the average matters

"Today it is $340" means nothing alone. Compared to yesterday and to the last seven days, it does. An example alert from the video reads: today $340, yesterday $385, 7 day average $370, below average, book now. That turns booking from a feeling into a comparison you can check.

## This is a different kind of agent

My other Muse Shorts are one time errands: a form, a part, a letter. This one is a scheduled job. It repeats every morning without a new prompt, and it only interrupts me when there is a decision to make. That is the pattern worth copying for anything with a moving price.

## The catch

- **An average is not a forecast.** A 7 day baseline tells you if today is cheap compared to last week. It cannot promise that tomorrow will not be cheaper.
- **A short window is a short memory.** Seven days of data does not show seasonal patterns. Use it to time a purchase you already plan to make, not to predict the market.
- **Check the fare before you pay.** Airfares change fast. Confirm the price on the booking page before you buy.
