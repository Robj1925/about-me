Meta Muse Has Changed My Life: How I Use It Every Day

I have used every major AI agent, and Meta Muse is the one that changed my daily life. Here is how I use it for errands, paperwork, big purchases, running my channel and learning, plus where it falls short.

✍️ By Robby J · 📅 November 8, 2026 · ⏱ 7 min read

Most people use an AI agent like a search box. I use Meta Muse like a member of my staff. I have subscriptions to Claude, Google, DeepSeek and Muse, and I started to use Muse only in the last month or so. It has saved me a lot of time, energy and money.

I am not sponsored by Meta, and I was not paid to make this video. This is a list of how I actually use it.

Errands and small stuff

The wrong toilet seat. At home I had a toilet seat that did not fit the toilet. I took a picture and asked Muse which part to get and where to get it. It looked at the picture, told me the seat was the wrong fit for that toilet, and told me which one I needed. Then I asked for a store near me. It found my local Home Depot and told me which location had it in stock. I went there, a worker confirmed it, and I bought it.

The appointment watcher. I need an appointment at a government office, and there were no open slots. So I had Muse set up a daily watcher. Every day at about 9:30 AM it checks for appointments and notifies me when there is an opening. Once it found a slot that did not work for me, so I told it to check again the next day, and the cycle repeats.

Two things make this work. Muse is on my computer and on my phone with the same memory, so the notification reaches me anywhere. And it runs in a loop: it keeps checking whatever you told it to check. And in my experience, its browser and computer use agent is much better than the ones in other agents like Claude.

The TV price check. I found a price for a TV and asked Muse to look for a cheaper one. It searched the web and came back with no cheaper price. But it told me that Black Friday was coming, so the price could drop.

Paperwork and admin

The scam letter. I got a letter that looked very real. It said I owed money and had to call a number so my warranty would not expire. I sent a picture to Muse and asked if it was real. It said right away that the letter had the telltale signs of a scam and that I should ignore it. It also searched the company name and showed me a report on an online scam tracker.

Inspection forms. An inspector has been checking my house, and I have to fill out forms that say I reviewed everything and made the fixes. I give Muse all the context, and it fills out the forms. I gave it access to my email, so it can also email the inspector. I review it, sign it, and it sends it.

The lost registration. I lost my car registration. I took pictures of the documents and the insurance, sent them to Muse, and asked it to book a DMV appointment. It could not book an appointment, so I have to go to the DMV in person. But it filled out the form I will need there, with all the required information, and told me exactly what to bring.

It can be wrong, and it can hallucinate. But I go in much better prepared than I would on my own.

The parking ticket. I got a parking ticket in the mail with a picture of my car. I sent it to Muse. It saw that my car lights were on in the picture, so I was not parked: I was picking up a friend. It wrote the appeal from that and sent it. All I did was take a picture of the letter.

Research for big decisions

A big purchase. I am looking into a big purchase, and I have used Muse for weeks to get the best value. It looks for lenders and compares interest rates and monthly terms. It checks listings, including Facebook Marketplace, and messages the sellers for me, also in other languages. It keeps everything in one private document, so it remembers what is going on.

Car rentals. It has messaged seven rental agencies for me so far, over WhatsApp and email. It asks about price, what is included and tax, and keeps it all in one running doc.

A group trip. I am planning a group trip, and Muse helps with the plan, the flights, the prices and everyone's availability. Every day, on its own, it checks the flight prices and sends a notification to my phone: today's price, yesterday's price, the seven day average, and whether I should buy now. It ends with a recommendation, not a list of links.

Running my channel

The model watcher. I have a daily watcher that looks for new models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Kimi and other big companies. Twice a day it tells me on my phone if anything new came out.

Sponsor emails. I had a pile of sponsor emails that I had ignored. Muse analyzed them, compared the offers, and keeps it all in one doc. Then it negotiates with the sponsors and reaches out to them directly. It does this every day on its own and asks me for my review. It runs on its own server, so my computer does not have to be on.

Learning

When I want to learn a topic, I ask Muse to make me a YouTube playlist of tutorials on it. I say "create me a YouTube playlist that teaches how to do this," and that is it.

Controlling Claude Code from my phone

Muse can control Claude Code or Antigravity for me from my phone. One day a video I had edited was still rendering when I had to leave. Later, from the Muse app on my phone, I told it the video should be ready and asked it to give the video to Claude and tell Claude to post it. Muse prompted Claude, told it where the video was, and Claude posted the video.

There is a lot of setup for this. You have to give the permissions to Claude, Muse, Antigravity and Chrome.

Where it falls short

  • It cannot get into every site. I tried to have it fill out an online application, and it could not get past the captcha.
  • It is not good at video editing. I had it try some edits for my content, and the results were not good at all.
  • It can be wrong. It can hallucinate, so check what it gives you before you sign or send.
