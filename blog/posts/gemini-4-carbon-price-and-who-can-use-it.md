## How it works

Google calls Carbon its strongest model for long horizon engineering work and cybersecurity. It is built for sustained agentic workflows, like hunting subtle bugs in huge codebases. Google says its teams "have seen Carbon run for days to persist through dead ends". That is an internal observation, not a benchmark.

- **Context:** up to 1M tokens, input and output combined (model card).
- **Model ID:** `gemini-4-carbon`
- **Intro price:** $2 per 1M input tokens and $10 per 1M output tokens, until November 30, 2026.
- **From December 1, 2026:** $4 per 1M input tokens and $20 per 1M output tokens.
- **Cached input:** 95% off the input price. AI Studio lists $0.10 per 1M now and $0.20 from December 1, with implicit caching only.
- **Versus Claude Fable 5.2:** Fable 5.2 costs $8 in and $40 out per 1M tokens. So Carbon is 4 times cheaper per token until November 30, and half the price after that.

## Who can use it

- **Developers:** the paid Gemini API in Google AI Studio. Carbon is not on the free tier.
- **Businesses:** Gemini for business.
- **Consumers:** it is rolling out to all Google AI Ultra subscribers in Google Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search.
- **Cyber defenders:** the Fairwind Program, where Carbon launched first.
- **Scientists:** Google offers a program with access to Argon and Carbon through Google Antigravity's highest usage tier, free for 6 months.

## Watch out

- The $2 and $10 price is an intro price. It ends November 30, 2026.
- "4 times cheaper" is per token only. Google says Carbon uses more tokens than Argon to explore, reason and verify, so a full task can cost more than the token price suggests.
- "Runs for days" is what Google's teams saw internally. It is not a product guarantee.
- The 1M context counts input and output together.
- I have no Gemini plan, so I did not test it myself. The demo is Google's.
