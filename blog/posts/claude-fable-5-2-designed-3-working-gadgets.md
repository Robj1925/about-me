## How it works

Anthropic gave Claude Fable 5.2 a one paragraph prompt: design and produce three working electronic devices with their own circuit boards, 3D printed enclosures and software. The partner was Diode, a company that designs and manufactures circuit boards.

The three devices:

1. A USB speakerphone
2. A home voice assistant hub
3. A handheld virtual pet

What Claude did, in Anthropic's words:

1. It wrote its own specifications.
2. It selected available electronic components from US distributors.
3. It routed each device's four layer circuit board.
4. It modeled the enclosures with computer aided design.
5. It programmed the devices' software.

Each board was ready for manufacturing in under a day, with minimal human input. When the routing got tangled, Claude said: "I should just rip these traces and start fresh."

Diode manufactured all three boards directly from Claude's design files, without any back and forth over adjustments. All three devices worked without needing any electrical modifications.

The same launch post says Fable 5.2 solved two open problems in theoretical physics. Both concern black holes.

## Who can use it

You cannot repeat this test at home. It needed Diode's factory, and it was a prerelease test by Anthropic.

You can use the model. Claude Fable 5.2 is on all Claude platforms and the API. It is not on the Free plan. Max plans get 1 to 3 Fable resets. Pro plans get Fable for one week, until November 16, 2026.

## Watch out

- These are prototypes. They have no electrical validation, antenna tuning, thermal testing or certification.
- "No electrical modifications" does not mean no humans. Diode engineers built, tested and assembled the devices, and Diode staff gave short prompts such as "clean up the schematic" and "continue".
- The launch post says one paragraph prompt for all three devices. The research post describes a simple prompt per device.
- Anthropic has a commercial relationship with Diode.
